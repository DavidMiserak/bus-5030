#+TITLE: Week 5 Discussion Post

** Initial Post: Management by Exception at Hen of the Wood

The variances requiring immediate attention are food cost (37%
vs. 30%), employee turnover (24% vs. 10%), customer satisfaction (82%
vs. 90%), and service time (18 vs. 12 minutes). Sales ($485,000
vs. $500,000) and labor cost (26% vs. 25%) are comparatively
minor. Not every unfavorable variance warrants investigation. Managers
should set thresholds, for example, deviations exceeding 5% or
$10,000. Food cost is 23% over target, turnover is 140% over target,
satisfaction is 8 points below target, and service time is 50% over
target. All are clearly significant.

The Downtown manager is not applying management by exception
effectively. Spending time on minor scheduling and routine purchases
prevents focus on the root cause: employee turnover. Three experienced
employees resigned, creating a cascade of problems. Inexperienced
staff likely waste food, work slower, and deliver poorer
service. These problems raise food cost, lengthen service time, and
lower customer satisfaction. Food cost, service time, and satisfaction
are symptoms; turnover is the root cause.

One advantage of MBE is that it frees senior managers for strategic
issues (Merchant, 1985). Two risks are that managers may overlook
gradual negative trends and that employees may hide problems to avoid
scrutiny (Merchant, 1985). Relying solely on financial thresholds can
also miss qualitative issues like morale (Kaplan & Norton, 2001).

Recommendations: (1) Implement a retention program with exit
interviews, competitive pay, and training, targeting turnover below
15% next quarter. (2) Delegate routine approvals to shift supervisors
and set variance thresholds. Investigate any deviation exceeding 5% or
$10,000, while tracking service time and satisfaction weekly.
