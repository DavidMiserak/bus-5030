## Framing Question

Assume Orion's operating income exceeded the static-budget target but
fell below the flexible-budget target. Should management consider the
quarter successful? Develop a balanced argument incorporating
financial performance, operating efficiency, sales growth, product
quality, customer satisfaction, and long-term strategy.

## Learning Objectives

- **Differentiate** static and flexible budgets and explain how each
  supports planning, control, and performance evaluation.
- **Prepare** static and flexible budget income statements using
  budgeted cost behavior and actual activity levels.
- **Calculate and classify** activity, revenue, cost, and
  operating-income variances as favorable or unfavorable.
- **Evaluate** managerial performance by separating activity-driven
  differences from variances tied to pricing, spending, efficiency,
  and controllable decisions.
- **Develop** evidence-based recommendations integrating variance
  analysis, nonfinancial measures, managerial accountability, and
  strategy.

## Scenario

Orion Manufacturing produces a specialized commercial air-filtration
unit. At the start of the quarter, management prepared a static budget
based on expected sales and production of **10,000 units**. Actual
production and sales were **12,000 units**.

- The CEO compared actual results to the original static budget and
  concluded costs were poorly controlled, since most actual expenses
  exceeded budget.
- The production manager disagreed, arguing the comparison is
  misleading because the company produced and sold 2,000 more units
  than planned.

You have been asked to prepare an independent analysis for the
executive leadership team.

### Static Budget Assumptions

| Budget Item                               |   Amount |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------: |
| Units produced and sold                   |   10,000 |
| Selling price per unit                    |      $80 |
| Direct materials per unit                 |      $24 |
| Direct labor per unit                     |      $15 |

## Required Components

1. Explain the purposes of static and flexible budgets, how the two
   differ, and the managerial circumstances in which each is most
   useful.
2. Prepare Orion's original static budget operating income statement
   at 10,000 units.
3. Prepare a flexible budget operating income statement at the actual
   activity level of 12,000 units.
4. Compare actual results with both the original static budget and the
   flexible budget at 12,000 units.
5. Calculate and classify each of the following, labeling every
   variance favorable or unfavorable:
    - Sales activity variance
    - Sales revenue variance
    - Variable-cost variances by cost category
    - Fixed-cost variances by cost category
    - Overall operating-income variance
6. Explain why comparing actual costs only with the static budget
   could lead management to an incorrect conclusion about the
   production manager's performance.
7. Distinguish variances caused by changes in activity from those
   caused by price, spending, or operational efficiency, and identify
   which are most likely within the production manager's control.
8. Evaluate the CEO's conclusion that costs were poorly controlled,
   supported by calculations and managerial-accounting principles.
9. Discuss at least **three limitations** of flexible-budget analysis
   (cost-behavior assumptions, relevant ranges, mixed costs, capacity
   constraints, quality, employee behavior, nonfinancial measures).
10. Recommend at least **three actions** for Orion's leadership
    addressing cost control, budgeting practices, managerial
    accountability, and future performance evaluation.

Additional expectations: include supporting schedules or clearly
formatted tables; write graduate-level analysis rather than defining
terms or reporting calculations; connect each recommendation directly
to evidence from the variance analysis.
