## Background

**Management by exception** is a control approach in which managers focus
attention on significant differences between actual performance and
established goals, budgets, or standards. Routine activities and minor
variations are handled by employees or lower-level supervisors, freeing senior
managers for issues requiring higher-level judgment. A small labor-cost
difference may need no intervention; a substantial cost overrun, sharp sales
decline, or persistent drop in customer satisfaction would warrant
investigation.

The approach improves efficiency, encourages delegation, and uses managerial
time better. It depends, however, on accurate reports, realistic performance
standards, and clearly defined thresholds for what counts as significant.
Managers must also weigh qualitative information, since an issue that looks
minor financially — employee dissatisfaction, declining product quality — can
become a serious long-term problem.

## Learning Objectives

- **Define** management by exception and explain its role in organizational
  control and decision-making.
- **Analyze** performance reports to distinguish significant variances from
  routine deviations.
- **Evaluate** favorable and unfavorable variances using quantitative and
  qualitative criteria.
- **Identify** root causes of performance problems and separate them from
  symptoms.
- **Develop** a corrective action plan that assigns responsibility,
  establishes measurable outcomes, and supports effective oversight.

## Scenario

You are the Regional Manager for Hen of the Wood restaurants, a company
operating two locations. Each restaurant manager is responsible for monthly
targets covering sales, food costs, labor costs, customer satisfaction, and
employee turnover. At quarter end you receive the following performance report
for the **Downtown** location.

| Performance Measure                 |     Target | Actual Result |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------: | ------------: |
| Monthly sales                       |   $500,000 |      $485,000 |
| Food cost as a percentage of sales  |        30% |           37% |
| Labor cost as a percentage of sales |        25% |           26% |
| Customer satisfaction score         |        90% |           82% |
| Average service time                | 12 minutes |    18 minutes |
| Employee turnover                   |        10% |           24% |

The restaurant manager explains that food prices rose unexpectedly, three
experienced employees resigned, and a new ordering system caused service
delays. The manager also reports spending significant time correcting minor
scheduling issues and approving routine purchases, leaving little time to
investigate food waste, employee dissatisfaction, and declining customer
satisfaction.

Senior leadership practices management by exception: managers should focus on
significant deviations from expected performance while routine operations and
minor variances are handled at lower organizational levels.

## Initial Post Prompt

1. Identify the performance variances that should receive the regional
   manager's immediate attention, and explain why each is significant.
1. Distinguish favorable from unfavorable variances. Should management
   investigate every unfavorable variance? Why or why not?
1. Recommend specific quantitative or qualitative thresholds Hen of the Wood
   could use to decide when a variance requires intervention.
1. Evaluate whether the Downtown manager is applying management by exception
   effectively. What responsibilities should be delegated to supervisors or
   employees?
1. Explain how the food-cost, employee-turnover, service-time, and
   customer-satisfaction variances may be connected. Which issue is the root
   cause, and which are symptoms?
1. Discuss one potential advantage and two possible risks of management by
   exception — for example, focusing only on negative results, overlooking
   gradual changes, encouraging employees to hide problems, or relying on
   inaccurate performance standards.
1. Provide two recommendations you believe will improve the location's
   financial results next quarter.
