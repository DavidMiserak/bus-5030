* Terms
+ Financial Accounting :: Branch of accounting focused on the business
  information needs of external users (creditors, investors,
  governmental agencies, financial analysts, etc.); its objective is
  to classify and record business events and transactions to produce
  external financial reports (income statement, balance sheet,
  statement of cash flows, and statement of changes in equity).

+ Managerial Accounting :: Branch of accounting focused on the
  information needs of managers and others working within the
  business. Its objective is to gather and report information that
  adds value to the business. Managerial accounting information is not
  regulated or reported to the public.

+ Overhead :: Costs associated with producing products or providing
  services that cannot be traced directly to those products or
  services in a cost-effective manner; includes indirect costs such as
  indirect materials; indirect labor; utilities; rent; depreciation on
  manufacturing facilities and equipment; and planning, design, and
  setup costs related to the product or service.

+ Value Chain Analysis            :: Involves analyzing a company’s activities to identify areas of value creation and improve competitive advantage.
+ Total Quality Management (TQM)  :: Aims at continuous improvement in all operations to enhance quality and customer satisfaction.
+ Activity-Based Management (ABM) :: Focuses on managing activities as the basis for improving customer value and profit.
+ Lean Accounting                 :: Seeks to minimize waste and maximize value for the customer, often leading to cost reduction and efficiency improvement.
+ Throughput Accounting           :: Concentrates on maximizing the rate at which a system generates money through sales.
+ Sustainability Accounting       :: Focuses on incorporating environmental and social costs into decision-making processes.
+ Risk Management                 :: Involves identifying, assessing, and managing risks to minimize their impact on the organization.

* Study Guide

Chapter 1 provides an overview of key terms and concepts that help set the foundation of managerial accounting concepts.

1) Managerial accounting users have different information needs than
   financial accounting users (see LO 1-1).

   + Managerial accounting
     1. Focuses on the needs of internal users (i.e., company managers).
     2. Uses economic, operating, and nonfinancial, as well as financial, data.
     3. Local (pertains to the company’s subunits).
     4. Limited by cost/benefit considerations.
     5. More concerned with relevance and timeliness, and is future oriented.

   + Financial accounting
     1. Focuses on the information needs of external users (i.e., external investors).
     2. More global than managerial accounting information. Information that applies to the whole company.
     3. Regulated by numerous authorities.
     4. Characterized by objectivity.
     5. Focuses on reliability and accuracy, and is historical in nature.

2) Both managerial and financial accounting are concerned with
   differentiating product cost from period cost.

   + Product costs are the costs incurred to make products and are
     composed of three different types of costs: direct materials,
     direct labor, and overhead (see LO 1-2).

     1. Both direct materials and direct labor are labeled direct cost
        because these types of product costs can be easily traced to
        the product in a cost-efficient manner.

     2. Overhead is often referred to as an indirect cost because
        these types of product costs are not easily traced to the
        product in a cost-efficient manner. Overhead costs include
        indirect materials, indirect labor, depreciation, rent, and
        utilities for manufacturing facilities. Overhead or indirect
        costs must be assigned to the cost of a product through a
        process called cost allocation.

   + Period costs comprise all the other costs incurred to operate the
     business unrelated to making products, also commonly referred to
     as selling, general, and administrative costs (see LO 1-2).

   + The accounting treatment of product costs and period costs
     differs (see LO 1-3).

     1. In a manufacturing company, as product costs are incurred,
        they are first accumulated into the Inventory account on the
        balance sheet. Product costs will remain in the Inventory
        account until the product is sold. At this time, product costs
        will be expensed on the income statement as cost of goods
        sold. This can cause product costs to be incurred in one
        accounting period, but expensed in a different accounting
        period. Any product costs remaining in the Inventory account
        at the end of the period are referred to as ending inventory.

     2. Service companies, like manufacturing companies, incur
        materials, labor, and overhead costs, but the products
        provided by service companies are consumed
        immediately. Therefore, service company product costs are not
        accumulated in an Inventory account, as they are immediately
        expensed on the income statement.
     3. In contrast, period costs are never accumulated on the balance
        sheet, but rather immediately expensed on the income statement
        in the period they are incurred. These costs are shown as
        selling, general, and administrative costs on the income
        statement.

   + Financial accountants need product cost information to determine
     the amount of inventory reported on the balance sheet and the
     amount of cost of goods sold reported on the income
     statement. Managerial accountants need to know the cost of
     products for pricing decisions and for control and evaluation
     purposes. When determining unit product costs, managers use the
     average cost per unit (see LO 1-2). Determining the actual cost
     of each product requires an unreasonable amount of time and
     recordkeeping and it makes no difference in product pricing and
     product cost control decisions.

3) All costs incurred to operate a business can be subdivided into
   three categories (see LO 1-4):

   + Upstream costs are costs that are incurred prior to the
     manufacturing process, including research and development costs
     and product design costs.

   + Midstream costs are costs incurred in the process of making
     products, including direct materials, direct labor, and
     manufacturing overhead.

   + Downstream costs are incurred after the manufacturing process,
     including marketing, distribution, and customer services.

4) In order for a company to be profitable, it must ensure that it has
   enough inventory on hand to provide to its customers. However,
   having too much inventory on hand can result in incurring
   unnecessary expenditures, such as excess inventory and inventory
   storage costs. A common method of avoiding these excess expenses is
   to utilize a just-in-time (JIT) inventory system (see LO 1-5). The
   goal of a JIT inventory system is to reduce the amount of inventory
   on hand by ordering inventory as the customer requests it. The
   downside of JIT inventory systems is that the delay between
   receiving inventory and delivering it to the customer can result in
   lost sales.

5) Understand the core concepts of the value chain, namely its role in
   creating products that meet customer needs. This is accomplished by
   mapping out the sequence of value-adding activities within an
   organization. Strategic cost management (SCM) describes the various
   strategies that companies employ across the value chain to control
   costs and enhance performance, aligning these strategies with the
   organization’s long-term goals and operational challenges. The
   success or failure of a strategy is often determined through a
   process called benchmarking (see LO 1-6).

6) A code of ethical conduct is needed in the accounting profession
   because accountants hold positions of trust and face conflicts of
   interest. In recognition of the temptations that accountants face,
   the IMA has issued the Statement of Ethical Professional Practice,
   which provides accountants guidance in resisting temptations and in
   making difficult decisions (see LO 1-7).

7) The Appendix discusses modern trends in managerial
   accounting. Current topics covered in this edition include
   artificial intelligence, cloud computing, data analytics, and many
   more. Many technological advancements are having a profound impact
   on industry and having a foundational understanding of these topics
   is important for all business majors (see LO 1-8).

* Questsions

1) What are some differences between financial and managerial
   accounting?

   + Financial accounting focuses on providing information to external
     users, such as investors and creditors, and is governed by
     standardized rules and regulations (e.g., GAAP or IFRS). It
     emphasizes historical data and the overall financial performance
     of the company. Managerial accounting, on the other hand, is
     aimed at internal users, such as managers, and focuses on
     providing relevant information for decision-making, planning, and
     control. It is more flexible, future-oriented, and may include
     non-financial data.

2) What does the value-added principle mean as it applies to
   managerial accounting information? Give an example of value-added
   information that may be included in managerial accounting reports
   but is not shown in publicly reported financial statements.

   + Value-added principle in managerial accounting refers to the
     concept of providing information that enhances decision-making
     and contributes to the overall value of the organization.  This
     information is often specific to internal operations and may not
     be relevant or required for external reporting.  An example of
     value-added information could be detailed cost analysis of
     individual products or services, which helps managers identify
     areas for cost reduction or process improvement.  This type of
     information is typically not included in publicly reported
     financial statements, which focus on aggregated financial
     performance.

3) What type of financial reporting describes the practice of tracking
   and reporting on environmental costs, investments in sustainable
   practices, and the financial implications of environmental
   regulations? (Appendix)

   + Environmental accounting or sustainability accounting describes
     the practice of tracking and reporting on environmental costs,
     investments in sustainable practices, and the financial
     implications of environmental regulations.  This type of
     reporting helps organizations assess their environmental impact,
     comply with regulations, and make informed decisions regarding
     sustainability initiatives.

4) How does product costing used in financial accounting differ from
   product costing used in managerial accounting?

   + Financial accounting is only concerned with the midstream costs
     (product costs) and how they affect the financial statements.
     Managerial accounting is concerned with all costs (upstream,
     midstream, and downstream) and how they affect the business.

5) What does the statement "costs can be assets or expenses" mean?

   + The purchase of an asset is a cost that is capitalized on the
     balance sheet and expensed over time through depreciation or
     amortization.  An expense is a cost that is immediately
     recognized on the income statement in the period it is incurred.
     The classification of a cost as an asset or expense depends on
     its nature and the accounting principles applied.

6) Why are the salaries of production workers accumulated in an
   inventory account instead of being expensed on the income
   statement?

   + GAAP based accounting requires that product costs, including the
     salaries of production workers, be accumulated in an inventory
     account on the balance sheet until the product is sold.  This is
     because these costs are directly associated with the production
     of goods and are considered part of the cost of inventory.  Once
     the product is sold, these costs are then expensed on the income
     statement as part of the cost of goods sold.

7) How do product costs affect the financial statements? How does the
   classification of product cost (as an asset vs. an expense) affect
   net income?

   + Product costs affect the financial statements by being recorded
     as assets in the inventory account on the balance sheet until the
     product is sold.  When the product is sold, these costs are
     transferred to the income statement as cost of goods sold, which
     reduces net income.  The classification of product costs as
     assets allows for the deferral of expenses until the related
     revenue is recognized, which can lead to higher net income in the
     period when the costs are incurred but not yet expensed.

8) What is an indirect cost? Provide examples of product costs that
   would be classified as indirect.

   + An indirect cost is a cost that cannot be easily traced to a
     specific product or service in a cost-effective manner.  Examples
     of product costs that would be classified as indirect include
     manufacturing overhead costs such as utilities, rent for the
     production facility, depreciation on manufacturing equipment, and
     salaries of production supervisors.

9) How does a product cost differ from a selling, general, and
   administrative cost? Give examples of each.

   + Product costs are costs incurred to create a product and include
     direct materials, direct labor, and manufacturing overhead.
     Examples of product costs include the cost of raw materials used
     in production, wages paid to assembly line workers, and factory
     rent.
   + Selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) costs are period
     costs that are not directly tied to the production of goods.
     Examples of SG&A costs include marketing expenses, office
     salaries, and administrative office rent.

10) Why is cost classification important to managers?

    + Managers use cost classification to make informed decisions
      about pricing, budgeting, and resource allocation.  By
      understanding the different types of costs (fixed, variable,
      direct, indirect, product, period), managers can analyze cost
      behavior, identify areas for cost control, and evaluate the
      profitability of products or services.  This information is
      crucial for strategic planning and operational efficiency.

11) What is cost allocation? Give an example of a cost that needs to
    be allocated.

    + Cost allocation is the process of assigning indirect costs to
      different cost objects (such as products, departments, or
      projects) based on a systematic method.  An example of a cost
      that needs to be allocated is the rent for a manufacturing
      facility that houses multiple production lines.  The rent would
      be allocated to each production line based on factors such as
      square footage occupied or machine hours used.

12) How has the Institute of Management Accountants (IMA) responded to the
    need for high standards of ethical conduct in the accounting
    profession?

    + The Institute of Management Accountants (IMA) has responded to
      the need for high standards of ethical conduct in the accounting
      profession by issuing the Statement of Ethical Professional
      Practice.  This statement provides guidance to accountants on
      how to handle ethical dilemmas and conflicts of interest,
      emphasizing principles such as honesty, integrity, objectivity,
      confidentiality, and professional competence.  The IMA also
      offers resources, training, and support to help accountants
      adhere to these ethical standards in their professional
      practice.

13) What are some of the common ethical conflicts that accountants
    encounter?

    + Accountants may encounter ethical conflicts such as pressure to
      manipulate financial results, conflicts of interest between
      personal gain and professional responsibilities, confidentiality
      breaches, and the temptation to overlook or misrepresent
      information.  Other common ethical conflicts include issues
      related to fraud, insider trading, and the misallocation of
      resources.  Accountants must navigate these conflicts while
      adhering to professional standards and maintaining their
      integrity.

14) What costs should be considered in determining the sales price of
    a product?

    + All costs that are directly associated with producing and
      selling the product should be considered in determining the
      sales price.  This includes direct materials, direct labor,
      manufacturing overhead (product costs), as well as selling,
      general, and administrative expenses (period costs).
      Additionally, managers may consider desired profit margins,
      market conditions, competitor pricing, and customer value
      perception when setting the sales price.

15) What is a just-in-time (JIT) inventory system? Name some inventory
    costs that can be eliminated or reduced by its use.

    + Just-in-time (JIT) inventory system is a management strategy
      that aims to increase efficiency and reduce waste by receiving
      goods only as they are needed in the production process, thereby
      minimizing inventory levels.  Some inventory costs that can be
      eliminated or reduced by its use include storage costs,
      insurance costs, obsolescence costs, and capital costs
      associated with holding excess inventory.

16) What does the term benchmarking mean? Name some reengineering
    practices. (LO 1-6)

    + Benchmarking is the process of comparing a company's processes,
      performance metrics, and practices to those of industry leaders
      or best-in-class organizations to identify areas for improvement
      and implement best practices.  Some reengineering practices
      include process redesign, workflow optimization, automation of
      repetitive tasks, and the implementation of new technologies to
      enhance efficiency and effectiveness.

17) What does the term activity-based management mean? (LO 1-6)

    + Activity-based management (ABM) is a management approach that
      focuses on analyzing and managing activities within an
      organization to improve customer value and profitability.  It
      involves identifying the activities that drive costs,
      understanding their relationship to products or services, and
      making informed decisions to optimize resource allocation,
      reduce waste, and enhance overall performance.

18) What is a value chain? (LO 1-6)

    + Linked sequence of activities that create direct value for customers.

19) What do the terms value-added activity and non-value-added
    activity mean? Provide an example of each type of activity. (LO 1-6)

    + A value-added activity is an activity that increases the worth
      of a product or service to customers, while a non-value-added
      activity does not contribute to customer value and may be
      considered wasteful.
      - Example of a value-added activity: Assembling a product that
        meets customer specifications.
      - Example of a non-value-added activity: Excessive paperwork or
        redundant approval processes that do not enhance the product
        or service.
