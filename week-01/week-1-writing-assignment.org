* Writing Assignment Overview: Upstream, Midstream, and Downstream Costs in the Oil and Gas Industry

The oil and gas industry is commonly divided into three major
operational segments: upstream, midstream, and downstream. Each
segment performs a distinct economic function and has a different cost
structure, risk profile, capital requirement, and exposure to market
conditions. Understanding these differences is essential for managers,
investors, policymakers, and energy professionals responsible for
evaluating projects and making strategic decisions.

* Task: Introduce, Analyze, Explain and Evaluate

For this assignment, write a 750–1,000-word analytical essay in APA
7th edition examining the major costs associated with upstream,
midstream, and downstream oil and gas operations. Your analysis should
move beyond simply defining each segment. Evaluate why costs differ
across the three segments, what factors drive those costs, and how
changes in costs influence profitability and strategic
decision-making.

** Upstream Costs: High Capital Risk + High Operating Variability

   + Cost 1: Drilling & Well Completion
     - Capital-intensive, highly variable by geology/depth/location
     - Ex-post uncertainty: drilling costs per well can swing dramatically
     - Risk driver: geological surprises, dry holes, unexpected conditions
     - Strategic implication: high project-level risk requires risk
       management (hedging, diversification, strict hurdle rates)
   + Cost 2: Production/Lifting Costs
     - Operational, recurring, but variable with depletion curve and volume
     - Predictable in form, unpredictable in magnitude (reservoir performance,
       decline rates)
     - Risk driver: reservoir productivity uncertainty, price sensitivity
     - Link: lifting costs compound the drilling risk -- poor wells cost more
       to operate
   + Quotes & Citations
     - Oil price dominance (risk): "Brent becomes the only factor explaining
       the movements in the upstream and midstream subsectors"
       (García-Amate et al., 2024, Abstract).
     - R&D/exploration investment: "Market players are investing in research
       & development (R&D) and technological advancements for developing
       high-performance, cost-efficient production" (MarketLine, 2026, p. 3).

** Midstream Costs: High Capital Investment, Predictable Operations

   + Cost 1: Pipeline Construction
     - Massive upfront capital (fixed, sunk cost)
     - But post-construction: throughput contracted, revenue stable
     - Risk driver: regulatory/environmental delays during construction;
       once built, utilization risk is low if contracts are in place
   + Cost 2: Pipeline Maintenance/Integrity
     - Predictable steady-state operating cost, relatively fixed
     - Regulatory requirement (not optional)
     - Risk driver: regulatory changes, rare catastrophic failures (low
       probability, high impact)
     - Strategic contrast to upstream: midstream shifts risk FROM operating
       variability TO capital/regulatory risk
   + Quotes & Citations
     - Oil price dominance (same as upstream): "Brent becomes the only
       factor explaining the movements in the upstream and midstream
       subsectors" (García-Amate et al., 2024, Abstract).
     - Note: midstream shares upstream's commodity price exposure despite
       different operational structure; this supports the capital-intensity
       thesis.

** Downstream Costs: Market-Driven + Periodic Capital

   + Cost 1: Crude Oil Feedstock
     - Direct material cost, commodity-priced, volatile, market-driven
     - Unpredictable (tied to global supply/demand, geopolitics)
     - Risk driver: price volatility, supply disruption
     - Mitigation strategy: hedging, long-term contracts, blending
   + Cost 2: Refinery Turnaround Expenses
     - Periodic planned capital (predictable timing, purpose)
     - Operational complexity, but known cost range (not like drilling surprises)
     - Risk driver: operational execution, scope creep (partially controllable)
     - Strategic contrast: feedstock risk is market risk; turnaround risk is
       operational/execution risk
   + Quotes & Citations
     - Downstream risk differs from upstream/midstream: "for most of the
       time the only factor in the downstream subsector is the market"
       (García-Amate et al., 2024, Abstract).
     - Interpretation: Unlike upstream/midstream (which are dominated by
       oil price), downstream has diverse risk factors, supporting the
       thesis that cost structures and risk profiles differ fundamentally
       across segments.

** Conclusion

   + Synthesis: upstream = highest risk, most variability; midstream =
     capital-intensive but operationally stable; downstream = market exposure
     but operational predictability
   + Strategic implication: each segment requires different risk management
     approaches, financing structures, and pricing strategies
   + Why it matters: capital allocation decisions depend on this risk
     differentiation; project finance structures differ by segment; margins
     and volatility expectations differ
   + Quotes & Citations
     - García-Amate et al. (2024) provides empirical support that the three
       subsectors have distinct risk profiles: upstream/midstream dominated
       by oil price (external risk), downstream by multiple market factors
       (operational complexity).

       dynamics to reinforce strategic importance of cost understanding.
