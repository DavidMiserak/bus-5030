#+TITLE: Week 3 Discussion Post

* Week 3 Discussion Post

Cost-plus pricing should remain only as a floor for predictable
compliance work; the firm should hold its $100,000 fee and move to
hybrid value-based pricing. Lowering to $92,000 would erode margin and
weaken premium positioning, especially when client benefit exceeds
$250,000.

The cost base should include engagement-specific direct costs such as
advisory labor, data feeds, project software, and analytics platforms,
plus allocated indirect costs such as cybersecurity, compliance,
technology infrastructure, and administrative support. Costs belong in
the base when they are traceable to the engagement, causally linked to
delivery, or material enough to affect pricing. A standardized 25%
markup is simple and ensures cost recovery, but it cross-subsidizes
complex, high-risk engagements, ignores willingness to pay, rewards
billable hours, reduces utilization efficiency, and discourages
innovation.

Cost-plus undercaptures specialized expertise, intellectual capital,
technology, and assumed risk. Competitors' $92,000 price creates
pressure, but clients receiving over $250,000 in benefit are less
price-sensitive; regulation and transparency requirements favor
documented outcomes over discounts. Value-based or hybrid pricing fits
customized, high-benefit advisory; fixed fees suit well-defined
compliance projects; subscriptions fit recurring monitoring. A hybrid
model combining a cost floor and a value premium can protect margin
and fund digital transformation while improving client alignment.

Management should monitor gross margin by engagement, price
realization, utilization, cost-to-serve, win rate, retention/churn,
and client ROI/satisfaction. Cost-plus remains appropriate only for
predictable, low-differentiation work; otherwise, value-based or
hybrid pricing better aligns firm and client value.
