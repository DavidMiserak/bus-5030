## Background

A **cost-plus pricing strategy** determines the total cost of providing a
product or service and adds a predetermined markup to set the selling price.
The cost base may include direct costs (labor, materials) and an allocation of
indirect costs or overhead. Example: an engagement costing $80,000 with a 25%
markup is billed at $100,000.

The method is simple and links costs, pricing, and profitability clearly. Its
limitation is that it looks inward at costs rather than at customer-perceived
value, competitive conditions, or willingness to pay. In financial services,
specialized expertise, risk management, technology, and intellectual capital
may create far more client value than delivery costs suggest, so firms often
blend cost-plus with market or value-based pricing.

## Learning Objectives

- **Explain** the principles of cost-plus pricing and its application in a
  financial services firm.
- **Analyze** direct and indirect costs to determine an appropriate cost base
  and markup for professional services.
- **Evaluate** the advantages and limitations of cost-plus pricing relative to
  value-based, fixed-fee, and other strategies.
- **Assess** how competition, client value, operating costs, profitability,
  and market conditions influence pricing decisions.
- **Develop and defend** a pricing recommendation using financial analysis,
  managerial accounting concepts, and strategic considerations.

## Scenario

A financial services firm provides specialized advisory, risk management,
regulatory compliance, and financial analysis services to corporate clients.
It has historically used cost-plus pricing, applying a standard profit margin
to the direct and indirect costs of each engagement.

Senior leadership now questions whether that remains appropriate:

- Operating costs have risen — compensation, technology investment,
  cybersecurity, and regulatory compliance.
- Competitors are moving to value-based pricing, subscription models, and
  fixed-fee arrangements.
- Clients demand greater price transparency and measurable business outcomes.

You are advising the firm's executive leadership team on pricing strategy.

## Prompt

To what extent should the firm continue using a cost-plus pricing model, and
under what circumstances might an alternative pricing strategy create greater
value for both the firm and its clients?

Consider:

1. How should the firm decide which direct and indirect costs belong in its
   cost base?
1. What are the advantages and limitations of a standardized markup applied
   across different services?
1. How might cost-plus pricing affect profitability, utilization, operational
   efficiency, and employee behavior?
1. Does cost-plus pricing adequately capture the value of specialized
   expertise, intellectual capital, technology, and assumed risk?
1. How could competitive conditions, client price sensitivity, regulation, and
   market positioning influence the decision?
1. When might value-based, fixed-fee, subscription, or hybrid pricing produce
   better financial outcomes?
1. What financial and nonfinancial metrics should management monitor to judge
   whether the pricing strategy creates sustainable value?
