## About the Course

### Course Description

This course will teach you to read, interpret, and analyze financial statements,
applying financial and managerial accounting concepts, tools, and techniques to
enhance strategic management thinking. Emphasizing a cross-functional
perspective, the course integrates internal accounting systems with managerial
decision-making across various organizational functions.

### Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course through the use of discussions,
readings, assignments, problem sets, projects, exams, and case studies, you
should be able to:

1. Analyze cost behavior and cost classifications to determine how fixed,
   variable, mixed, direct, and indirect costs affect managerial
   decision-making.

2. Apply cost-volume-profit (CVP) analysis to evaluate break-even points,
   target profits, operating leverage, and the effects of changes in costs,
   prices, and sales volume.

3. Evaluate product and service costs using job-order costing, process costing,
   activity-based costing, and other relevant cost-allocation approaches.

4. Develop and analyze operating and financial budgets to support
   organizational planning, coordination, resource allocation, and managerial
   control.

5. Use relevant-cost analysis for managerial decisions, including special
   orders, make-or-buy decisions, product-line decisions, outsourcing, and
   decisions involving constrained resources.

6. Analyze organizational performance using standard costs and variance
   analysis, identifying the causes and managerial implications of favorable
   and unfavorable variances.

7. Evaluate decentralized organizational performance using responsibility
   accounting, return on investment, residual income, transfer pricing, and
   appropriate financial and nonfinancial performance measures.

8. Integrate quantitative and qualitative information into strategic managerial
   decisions, considering profitability, risk, ethical implications,
   sustainability, and long-term organizational objectives.

### Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI)

#### Open Use

AI use is permitted in this course with attribution. I am all for using
technology tools to make our lives easier and faster; for instance, Excel,
Grammarly, and Alexa are some of my favorites. At the same time, please remember
that part of the college course assesses your understanding of the material –
not AI’s understanding. Therefore, my artificial intelligence (AI) policy for
this class is as follows:

#### Acceptable Uses of AI

1. **Research & Learning**: Use AI to gather information, summarize research,
   and explain topics.

2. **Brainstorming**: AI can help generate ideas and understand complex
   concepts.

3. **Writing Assistance**: Use AI for grammar checks, style editing, and
   content organization. The final content must be original.

#### Unacceptable Uses of AI

1. **Completion**: Submitting AI-generated work as your own, including
   discussion posts and projects is prohibited.

2. **Assessments:** Using AI to generate answers for quizzes or exams is not
   allowed.

3. **Plagiarism**: Do not rephrase or paraphrase sources using AI without
   proper citation. Please attribute any AI-generated text appropriately.

4. **Misrepresentation**: Presenting AI-generated ideas or analysis as your own
   is considered academic dishonesty.

#### Limitations of AI & Your Responsibilities

It is important to recognize that AI has inherent limitations, and responsible
AI usage requires human oversight and verification. If you choose to use AI in
assisting you with your projects/assignments you are responsible for:

1. Verifying the quality, appropriateness, integrity, and impartiality of the
   AI-generated output before submitting the assignment.

2. Disclosing any AI-generated material used and providing proper attribution,
   including in-text citations, quotations, and references.

#### PLEASE NOTE

All final work you submit must reflect your own individual thoughts and efforts.
If you forget to properly attribute your use of AI tools in your assignments
(like ChatGPT) it will be considered a violation of the college’s academic
honesty policy.
